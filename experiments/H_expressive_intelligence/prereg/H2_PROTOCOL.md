# H2: human perception of NERVA's expressive movement (protocol draft, 2026-10-12)

**Status: PENDING.**
- No participant has been recruited.
- No data have been collected.
- No ratings exist.

Programme H reports **claim B (interpretable expression) as untested** until this protocol has (1) an ethics
determination and (2) the user's explicit authorization to collect data.

Model-generated or LLM-generated ratings are never used as a substitute. An automated observer may be reported only
as an exploratory analysis, clearly labelled.

## 1. Ethics and authorization (must happen first)

- **Is review required?** This is research with human participants:
  - participants view videos and give ratings and free text;
  - it may involve online recruitment and payment.

  At essentially every university such work needs either research-ethics-committee (REC/IRB) approval or a formal
  exemption issued **by the institution**. A researcher cannot self-certify an exemption. Recruitment platforms
  (e.g. Prolific) also require an ethics reference.
- **Determination for programme H:** formal review or an institutional exemption decision **is required before
  any recruitment**. Programme H cannot obtain it. The user (the researcher of record) must submit this protocol to
  their institution's ethics office.
- **Before any data are collected:**
  1. written approval or exemption, with a reference number recorded in `DEVIATIONS.md`/`RESEARCH_LOG.md`;
  2. the user's explicit go-ahead in writing;
  3. the final stimulus set frozen with SHA-256 hashes;
  4. this protocol and its analysis plan committed (frozen).
- **Participants:**
  - adults (18+), informed consent, the right to withdraw;
  - no personal identifiers collected (platform IDs are used only for payment and are not stored with the
    responses);
  - fair compensation at the platform's recommended rate;
  - a debrief explaining that the robot has no feelings and that the descriptors are experimental labels.

## 2. Questions

1. Can observers identify the intended movement quality of each H1 style better than chance?
2. Do the identifications survive matched speed?
3. Are the designed styles more recognisable and rated more natural, coherent and intentional than random-parameter
   styles (X2) and neutral motion (X0)?
4. Do observers agree with each other (inter-rater reliability), and do they agree with the designer's labels?
5. (Later, H4/H6.) Can observers tell which of two identically placed robots had which kind of history? This uses
   paired clips from the "most important test", without any history information in the clip.

## 3. Stimuli

The stimuli are generated by `scripts/h2_stimuli.py` from the H1 configurations after the H1 gate passes.
- **Content:** MuJoCo renderings of NERVA in one fixed scene:
  - a flat grey floor;
  - a single orange ball as the target (a neutral object, not an agent);
  - the same lighting throughout.
- **Camera:**
  - one fixed oblique camera (azimuth 120°, elevation −20°, distance 1.8 m, aimed at the start–target midpoint);
  - 480 × 360 px, 25 fps;
  - no text, labels, colours or sound that differ between conditions.
- **Clip length:** each clip shows one approach from the start to 5 s after arrival, at most 45 s (slow styles take up to ≈ 35 s at the progress floor of 0.029 m/s).
- **Clip set (≈ 72 clips):**
  - 6 designed styles (X1) × 2 task geometries × 2 seeds = 24;
  - matched-speed versions of the 6 styles × 2 × 2 = 24;
  - neutral X0 × 2 × 2 = 4;
  - 20 random-style X2 clips.
- **Seeds and selection:** seeds come from partition X; clips are chosen by a fixed rule (seed index 0 and 1), not
  for how they look.
- **Hashes:** the hash list is committed before recruitment.

## 4. Procedure (online, within-subjects, ≈ 25 min)

- **Training:** 2 neutral clips, so participants learn what the robot is and can do. No style names are shown.
- **Main block:** each participant sees a random subset of 36 clips:
  - balanced: every style appears 3–4 times, with matched-speed and native-speed versions mixed;
  - order randomised per participant, with no two clips of the same style in a row.
- **Per clip:**
  1. **Forced choice:** "Which word best describes how the robot moved?" Options: cautious, assertive, curious,
     hesitant, relaxed, energetic, neutral / none of these. Option order is randomised per participant.
  2. **Continuous ratings** (0–100 sliders): caution, confidence, curiosity, hesitancy, relaxedness, energy.
  3. **7-point ratings:** naturalness, movement coherence ("the movements fit together"), intentionality ("the
     robot seemed to be doing something on purpose").
  4. **Optional free text:** "In a few words, how would you describe this robot's behaviour?"
- **Quality control:**
  - 2 attention checks (an instruction clip);
  - completion-time and straight-lining exclusion rules, fixed in advance.

## 5. Sample size and analysis (fixed before data)

**Chance baseline:** 1/7 = 0.143 for forced choice.

**Primary model:** a mixed-effects logistic regression of hit (choice = intended style).
- Fixed effects: style.
- Random intercepts: participant and clip.
- One-sided test per style of hit probability > 1/7, Holm across the 6 styles.

**Target N = 60 completed participants (recruit up to 72 to allow for exclusions).**

Design power was simulated with `scripts/h2_power.py` (1,000 simulated designs, no participant data). Setup:
- 3 trials per style per participant per speed condition, from 4 clips per style;
- random intercepts on the logit scale for participants and clips;
- per-participant one-sided t-test (a conservative stand-in for the mixed model), Holm across 6 styles.

| N | true hit rate | participant SD | clip SD | power per style | power to detect all six |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 60 | 0.30 | 0.5 | 0.5 | 0.98 | 0.89 |
| 60 | 0.25 | 0.5 | 0.5 | 0.89 | 0.58 |
| 40 | 0.30 | 0.5 | 0.5 | 0.94 | 0.77 |
| 60 | 0.30 | 0.5 | 1.0 | 0.92 | 0.65 |

N = 60 is adequate for hit rates around 0.30. Weaker effects (0.25), or large clip-to-clip variability, need more
participants or more clips per style. The final N is fixed from a pilot with ≤ 10 participants, run only after
approval, using this script.

**A style counts as "recognised" if both hold:**
- (a) its hit rate is above chance (Holm);
- (b) its target-quality rating exceeds the neutral clips' rating by ≥ 10 points (95% CI lower bound > 0).

**Matched speed:** the same criteria within the matched-speed clips. A style recognised only at native speed is
reported as **speed-carried**.

**Speed confound:** ratings regressed on the measured progress speed of each clip. The style effect is reported
over and above speed.

**Reliability:**
- Krippendorff's α for forced choice;
- ICC(2,k) for each continuous scale;
- the agreement pattern shown as a confusion matrix.

**Designed vs random:** naturalness, coherence and intentionality of X1 vs X2, using a mixed model.

**Free text:**
- coded by two human coders blind to condition, using a codebook fixed after piloting on 10% of responses;
- Cohen's κ reported;
- no automated coding counts as primary.

## 6. What a result would and would not show

- **A pass** shows that these motion programmes communicate the qualities to this observer population in this
  context. It does not show the robot has the corresponding states.
- **A failure** limits programme H to claim A (movement differences) and leaves the architecture decision's
  interpretability column empty.
